The world lost Dolly Parton this week. Tributes are pouring in for her music, her generosity, her decades of chosen kindness. But there's a smaller story worth telling here, one that speaks directly to families like ours.

Dollywood was among the first theme parks anywhere to build a **calming room** — a quiet space for guests with autism to step away from sensory overload without having to leave the park entirely. Staff had noticed the need every single day, especially through the busy summer months, and the park's ride-accessibility team partnered with Autism Speaks to build something real.

That's not the kind of advocacy that trends on social media. There was no campaign, no press tour built around it. It's just a room — soft lighting, low noise, somewhere to breathe. But if you've ever stood in a crowded park watching your child's nervous system reach its limit, you know exactly what that room is worth. It's the difference between packing up and going home, and staying for the rest of the day.

You can [watch a short tour of the room here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ml7Io-EWWdU) — beanbag chairs, a pop-up hideaway tent, low lighting, and quiet, all tucked away from the noise of the park.

That's what good inclusion usually looks like, in my experience. Not a statement. Not a hashtag. Just someone noticing a real need and quietly making space for it.

Rest easy, Dolly. Thank you for making room for families who need it.
